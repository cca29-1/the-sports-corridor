UNDERSTAND THE SCIENCE

How does the body resynthesise ATP?

ATP provides the immediate usable energy for muscle contraction. The body continually resynthesises ATP through multiple pathways working simultaneously. Which system contributes most depends on intensity, duration, the performer and other conditions.

ATP-PC system

Rapid ATP resynthesis using phosphocreatine stored in muscle. Important during very short, explosive efforts. Its capacity is limited.

High power · Low capacity

Anaerobic glycolytic system

Resynthesises ATP by breaking down carbohydrate without directly requiring oxygen. Important during demanding efforts as phosphocreatine availability falls.

Relatively rapid ATP supply

Aerobic system

Resynthesises ATP using oxygen-dependent processes. Supports sustained activity and becomes increasingly important as exercise continues.

High capacity · Lower maximum rate
Common misconception: The systems do not switch on and off in sequence. All three contribute from the start; their relative contributions change.
