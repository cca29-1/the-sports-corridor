With drag enabled, the app uses a deliberately simplified quadratic resistance model. Real balls, shots and javelins have more complex aerodynamic behaviour. No calculation here represents measured athlete performance.

What controls a projectile's path?

1. Release velocity

Higher release speed normally increases the distance a projectile travels, if other factors remain constant. Velocity has horizontal and vertical components.

2. Release angle

A greater angle increases the initial vertical component but decreases the horizontal component at a fixed release speed. At equal release and landing heights, with no air resistance, 45° gives the greatest range.

3. Release height

A greater release height generally gives the projectile more time to travel horizontally before landing. The ideal range-maximising angle can therefore be less than 45°.

4. Gravity and drag

Gravity accelerates the projectile downward at approximately 9.81 m/s². Air resistance opposes motion and can reduce its range. Real aerodynamic forces depend on the object and conditions.

Connect the science

Horizontal velocity

vₓ = v cos θ — stays constant when air resistance is neglected.

Vertical velocity

vᵧ = v sin θ at release, then decreases by 9.81 m/s each second without drag.

y = h + (v sin θ)t − ½gt²

🏀 Basketball — accuracy matters

Investigate the trajectory required for a ball to reach the hoop. Changing release speed, angle or height alters the ball's height as it crosses the hoop's horizontal position. Reaching that position alone does not guarantee a basket.

⚫ Shot put — maximise distance

Explore how release velocity, angle and height affect distance. The optimum release angle is not necessarily 45° because the shot starts above its landing height.

⚽ Football — balance distance and height

Compare a driven pass with a high clearance. Can you increase horizontal range without producing an excessively high trajectory?

↗ Javelin — a model has limits

Use the point-projectile model to explore basic launch mechanics, then evaluate why a real javelin's lift, drag, orientation and angle of attack make its flight more complex.

Show guidance

Extended application

Question 2

Evaluate why a basketball player should not simply use a 45° release angle for every shot.

Show indicative points

Consider release height, distance to the basket, target height, release speed, entry angle, defensive pressure and the need for accuracy. The familiar 45° result only maximises ideal range when release and landing heights are equal, with no drag.

Test a hypothesis

Use the simulator to gather evidence for this statement:

“45° is always the best release angle in sport.”
  1. Set release height to 0 m and disable drag.
  2. Keep release speed fixed; compare launch angles.
  3. Repeat with release height at 2 m.
  4. Consider whether accuracy, rather than maximum range, matters in basketball.
